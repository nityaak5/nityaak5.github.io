**TL;DR:** I went for a walk (again) and had feelings about everything.

> If you are here expecting another post about AI evaluation or whatever technical rabbit hole I have been into lately, feel free to scroll past this one. My friends have told me a million times that I should stop mixing my personal and work posts. Have they made compelling arguments? Yes. Do I listen to them? No.

I was out today and I felt it again. That little crunch under my shoes, the different shades of yellow and orange slowly taking over, fall being around the corner, and that glistening grass from September rain (if you know, you know).

I just checked, and it has been a little over a year since I first wrote about Nijmegen here. And surprise surprise, I am moving places again, third time in these three years. Perhaps you will get another perspective when I move closer to the Goffert Park in the coming days. I hope it will be just as good. I already know the sunsets are going to be promising from the west-facing window in my living room, so I am taking that as a good sign.

This post might not be the happiest one. Maybe it is more of a looking back at the year since I first wrote about Nijmegen, because a lot has changed since then, especially in the past month. So much that I can barely keep track of everything happening around me. But you know what is still constant?

_The charm of this old city._

Worry not, I won’t bore you again with my obsession with sunsets by the river Waal, even though I WANT TO. Instead, today, let me take you through the streets of _Lange Hezelstraat_. One of the busiest, and perhaps one of the prettiest streets of Nijmegen. But first, how did I even end up there? In fact, this last week, I have been there three times.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*JN52H35iySuH7EqWjuPIxw.jpeg)

Today there was a bit of sunshine, and I decided to make the most of it. I stepped out for the Saturday market to get some cookies from _‘t Stoepje._ I was a bit late, but I did manage to find myself some blueberry muffins. It was busy and there was a couple who had just gotten married, stepping out of the church. I had a little smile when I saw them. Then almost immediately I saw someone being taken away in an ambulance and my heart sank. These days, anything hospital-related seems to affect me a little more than usual. And then I saw a guy playing an accordion, and somehow I was back to simply enjoying the afternoon. That was a rollercoaster of emotions, I tell you.

Later, I decided to go to the _Kronenburgerpark_. I found a spot by the pond, blueberry muffin in my hand. I hadn’t realised how long it had been since I had visited this park, or how many little memories I had collected here, especially since last fall. On my left was the bench where I once sat talking about bird communication with someone, and when it suddenly started to rain, we took shelter under two giant trees and somehow ended up discussing ethics in cybersecurity. Then there were the quiet moments of a first meet with someone, where we tried to fill the silences by staring at the ducks in the pond. And this spot, exactly where I was sitting right then, was where my flatmates and I had a picnic. Ah, that quiet ache of reminiscence.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*bHCer1pNGLVuJFfknPn1Ig.jpeg)I had already taken a bite :p

I read my book, ate my blueberry muffin, traced the little patterns in the leaves and watched the water for a while. There is something about doing absolutely nothing that makes you notice the smallest things again. The veins running through a leaf, the way the water catches the light, the tiny patterns nature seems to make without trying. Maybe that is where the childlike wonder comes from, being curious about things that are completely ordinary and somehow finding them fascinating anyway. The church bells rang at 5 pm, which felt like my cue to head back home. I had planned to take the bus, but missed it, so I walked instead. And that is how I ended up on the _Lange Hezelstraat_.

There was so much hustle and bustle. Fancy restaurants, little shops, people walking around, music coming from somewhere. I came across that record store from last year, when a friend was visiting. I remember we chatted with an old man there (the owner) and I was genuinely surprised that he knew what _Claude_ was. He wasn’t in the store today. I wonder where he is.

I walked a little further. There was the place where I got a very random haircut a few days ago, which alarmed the hairdresser because she thought it was an abrupt decision. A few steps later, someone was singing _Wonderwall_ by Oasis, and I was reminded of someone I used to know. Then I saw the flower market closing. Ah man, I love those sunflowers. I probably won’t see them in a long time now.

Had you told me last year what this year would look like, I wouldn’t have believed it. There have been more goodbyes than I had expected, and perhaps one day Nijmegen will be on that list. But for now, I am glad that day is not today. I don’t quite know what the next few months will look like, but strangely, it doesn’t scare me. If anything, I am excited to find out.

Maybe that is also why I have grown so fond of this place. Nijmegen takes me on walks, gives me sunsets, lets me sit by a pond and wonder about absolutely nothing, and somehow always gives me something new to notice. It has seen quite a few versions of me and has never once disappointed me. This reminds me of a conversation I had with my friends recently: _‘Why don’t you date?’_ , and honestly, when you put it that way, a boy has a lot to compete with.

I think today was one of those days where I actually sat with myself for a bit. Not in a dramatic, let-me-figure-out-my-life kind of way, but just quietly. Sometimes that is enough, I guess.

Oh, and I had one large, perfectly round aloo paratha for dinner tonight. ❤️
