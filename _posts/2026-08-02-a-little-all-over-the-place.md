![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*P6FqB0w0uRkfcA93QE98GA.avif)

A couple of weeks ago, I was completely overwhelmed with everything happening around me. I had just come back from my trip to Italy. Now, a trip is supposed to leave you feeling refreshed, right? It was quite the opposite for me. I had an amazing time, but I don’t think I ever really clocked out of work before leaving, and as soon as I got back, I was working again. By then, I was mentally and emotionally exhausted. It felt like everything around me was falling apart. The strange part was that I knew exactly why. In fact, I knew all the reasons.

Over the last few years, I have become much better at recognising patterns in myself. I can usually tell which thought leads to which emotion, which situation triggers what reaction, and how one thing slowly spirals into another. I think moving to the Netherlands played a part in that. But even though I knew every reason for every emotion I was feeling, it did not make dealing with it any better. Knowing why I felt overwhelmed didn’t stop me from feeling overwhelmed. Social media certainly didn’t help either. My Instagram was still full of stories from Italy while I was sitting in the library near my house, staring at my laptop and unable to focus on anything. It got so bad that I reached for my ultimate coping mechanism: my dear diary. I wrote about everyone and everything that was bothering me at that moment. It wasn’t 18 pages, front and back, but it was definitely more than ten.

The next day, I got my period. It all made sense. Funny. Every single time.

A few days later, everything felt normal again. I picked up reading after what I consider a long break ( 5 whole days, can you imagine?). My flatmate had recently finished _Tuesdays with Morrie_ , and I remembered reading it in high school. Back then, I thought it was a sweet book. Reading it ten years later felt completely different. It was like wrapping myself in a warm blanket. Somehow, it managed to put words to emotions I had been carrying around for weeks.

Interestingly, while I was sitting with this feeling of comfort from a book, I found myself doing something that completely contradicted it. I started reading reviews.

Somewhere between reading _Tuesdays with Morrie_ and my existential crisis the week before, I had also read another article, about [how to have an original thought](<https://feifeiwrites.substack.com/p/how-to-start-having-original-thoughts>). It talked about how, because of social media, our stance on a particular thing is often not entirely our own but influenced by what people around us think. I have noticed this behaviour in myself at times. If I come across something controversial, my first instinct is to check the comments. Why do I do that? To hide behind the majority? But then, I have also had plenty of unpopular opinions in my life. So maybe it is not about hiding. Maybe it is about wanting to know where everyone else stands only out of curiosity. Anyway, after I finished _Tuesdays with Morrie_ , I re-read some reviews of the book and felt so disappointed when people called it boring or said it wasn’t that great. A part of me immediately agreed. But then I caught myself being influenced by the comments section, thanks to the article I had just read.

I find myself doing this often now. Whenever I catch myself jumping to the comments before forming my own opinion, I stop. It is difficult, but I think it is important.

Why did I ramble about all of this? I don’t really know. Maybe I just like rambling.

I am listening to the entire _Red_ album by Taylor Swift while writing this. I thought about having coffee a while ago, and my flatmate just made some right now. I feel good today, probably because I am in my follicular phase. Did I just tell the internet about my menstrual cycle? Definitely. Will I cringe out when I read this a year from now? Quite possibly.

Perhaps you’ll find it funny, perhaps not.

I think I’ll stop here.

Until next time.

Nityaa
